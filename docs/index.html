City Experimentation and Learning Lab

Cities as places to experiment and learn

CEL Lab is an urban economics and policy research group at IIT Roorkee. We treat cities as places to experiment and learn: building empirical evidence on urban growth, land, housing, infrastructure and local governance, and working with city governments to test what actually improves urban outcomes.

Projects

Current projects

Project details →
Co-PI

Assessment of Financial Resources of Local Self-Governing Institutions (PRIs and ULBs) in Uttarakhand

A state-wide assessment of the revenue base, expenditure patterns and fiscal capacity of Uttarakhand's panchayati raj institutions and urban local bodies, identifying constraints on own-source revenue and options for strengthening local finances.

Theme: Municipal finance, property tax and urban sprawl
PI

Taxing Vacant Land: A Pan-India Panel Study of Property Tax Discounts and Urban Sprawl in Metropolitan and Tier-2 Cities

Builds a multi-city, multi-year panel of property-tax rules and rates across Indian metropolitan and tier-2 cities to examine how concessions on vacant land affect land holding, development timing and the outward spread of cities.

Theme: Municipal finance, property tax and urban sprawl, Land use optimisation for sustainable urbanisation
PI

Property Tax, Land Records and Urban Growth in Nagar Nigam Roorkee: A Plot-Level Assessment and Policy Options

A plot-level study of Roorkee's property-tax register and land records, matched to GIS and satellite footprints, to estimate the gap between taxable and taxed property, trace how growth is occurring across the city, and set out policy options for the municipal corporation.

Theme: Municipal finance, property tax and urban sprawl, Urban growth simulation

Population Synthesis for Disaggregated Travel Demand Modelling

Generates synthetic populations of households and individuals for Indian cities — matching census marginals and survey microdata — as the input to activity- and agent-based travel demand models that work at the level of individual trips rather than zonal aggregates.

Theme: Commuting behaviour, built environment and neighbourhood typologies
